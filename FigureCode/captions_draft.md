## Fig. 2

Fig. 2 | PBMT score coverage and concordance with PlastChem. (a) Availability of accepted computational and mapped PlastChem scores among 15,125 compounds. Bars partition the full inventory into four mutually exclusive categories: computational only, shared, PlastChem only and neither. (b–e) Paired score comparisons for toxicity, bioaccumulation, mobility and persistence, with shared n = 2,963, 270, 135 and 234, respectively. Bars above the matrices show exact agreement, nonzero |Δ| ≤0.5, 0.5<|Δ|≤1 and |Δ|>1, from left to right. Numerical labels are shown only for counts of at least 2 and at least 2.5% of the largest cell count within each matrix; unlabelled cells may therefore contain nonzero counts. Outlines mark exact score agreement. Shading represents within-domain fractions, scaled separately for each domain. Mean |Δ| is the mean absolute ordinal-score difference. Bioaccumulation uses selected QSAR BCF evidence and excludes the structural halogen rule. Missing scores are excluded from paired comparisons; supported zeros remain valid. These descriptive comparisons do not establish predictive accuracy.

Fig. 4 | Composition of the 101 Level 1 compounds. (a) Counts by primary use, retaining one supplied primary-use assignment per compound. (b) Counts by nine mutually exclusive structure-based display classes derived from recorded SMILES using the retained ordered classification rules; X denotes F, Cl, Br or I. (c) Counts by GIC regulatory annotation in the source database. All panels include the same 101 unique compounds satisfying H ≥12 and E ≥12 under the figure-only five-level classification, using the existing separately rounded domain scores. Each panel totals 101; the zero-based count axes have panel-specific ranges. General-use annotations do not establish functions specific to plastics, and GIC annotations do not constitute a current jurisdiction-specific legal assessment. These are descriptive counts; scores, ranks and Top-n membership are unchanged.

## Fig. 5

Fig. 5 | Sensitivity of five-level priorities to weights and classification thresholds. (a) Jaccard similarity between baseline and perturbed Level 1 or Level 1 + 2 lists. Each component weight is reduced or increased by 20% separately; each point shows the lower similarity of the two directions for that cohort. Rows are ordered by the lower of the two cohort values. The x axis begins at 40%. (b) Agreement with baseline under separately applied alternative assumptions: equal PBMT weights, equal exposure weights, halved inventory weight, or a hazard/exposure vH cutoff of 11 or 13 instead of 12. Level agreement is the percentage of compounds retaining the same five-level class; list Jaccard is intersection divided by union, including both departures and entrants. All displayed values are percentages. The baseline contains 15,125 compounds, with 101 in Level 1 and 1,220 in Levels 1 + 2. In weight scenarios, the affected domain is rescaled to a theoretical maximum of 16 before half-up rounding. Cutoff alternatives apply to rounded domain scores; the other boundaries remain 4 and 8. Evidence and missing-data treatment are fixed. These deterministic sensitivity scenarios do not establish optimal weights or provide confidence intervals.

## Extended Data Fig. 3

Extended Data Fig. 3 | GIC regulatory concordance and sensitivity to component weights. (a) Distribution of recorded GIC categories within the five priority levels. Parentheses indicate the number of compounds in each level; each compound is assigned its highest-precedence recorded category. (b) Capture of compounds annotated as prohibited/phase-out or restricted/authorisation under four ranking approaches at a fixed screening budget of 5% of the 15,125-compound inventory. Capture is the percentage of all 1,451 compounds with these annotations that are selected, rather than their percentage within the screened subset. The dashed line marks the integrated-score baseline of 16.99%. (c) Capture after separately decreasing or increasing each component weight by 20%. Values in parentheses show changes from baseline in percentage points; blue and warm shading indicate decreased and increased capture, respectively. Percentages and changes are rounded independently. In panels b and c, boundary score ties receive equal fractional selection weights, giving 756.25 expected selections. Perturbed domain scores are rescaled to a theoretical maximum of 16 before half-up rounding; inventory omission rescales the remaining exposure inputs. Evidence and missing-data treatment are fixed, and official weights, scores and ranks are unchanged. Because GIC inventory evidence also contributes to scoring, these comparisons describe concordance rather than independent validation or optimal weights. An unmatched GIC record does not establish absence of regulation.
